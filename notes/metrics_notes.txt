
Do not judge a run from a single raw metric curve. PPO metrics are noisy.
Prefer rolling averages over 50-200 training updates.

Useful first plots:

- accumulated_reward, rolling mean
- entropy_gauss
- log_std_mean
- explained_variance
- loss_v
- approx_kl
- clip_frac
- ratio_std

A healthy run often looks like:

- accumulated_reward increases over time
- entropy_gauss decreases slowly or stabilizes
- log_std_mean decreases slowly or stabilizes
- explained_variance rises toward positive values
- approx_kl stays below target_kl most of the time
- clip_frac is not always zero and not constantly very high
- ratio_mean stays close to 1.0
- ratio_std stays small/moderate
- grad_norm is finite and does not explode

For this project, compare runs by reward and behavior first. Loss going down is
not enough. A lower loss can simply mean the critic is predicting small returns
or the policy update is very small.


Metric reference
================

training_number
---------------

Meaning:
Sequential number of the PPO update. It starts at 1 and increases every time
train_on_buffer() successfully trains on collected data.

Calculated as:
self.training_count += 1 in train_on_buffer().

How to interpret:
This is the x-axis for training-update plots. It is not the same as environment
step count or episode count.

Rising:
Normal. Training is progressing.

Problems:
If this stops increasing, the buffer is not reaching a training condition or
training has stopped/crashed.


accumulated_reward
------------------

Meaning:
Sum of all rewards currently stored in the PPO buffer before the update.

Calculated as:
sum(self.buf.rew_buf[:buffer_sample_count])

How to interpret:
Higher is usually better, but be careful: this is not normalized by sample_count.
If one update has more samples than another, accumulated_reward can be larger
just because the buffer contained more transitions.

Best use:
Plot a rolling mean. Also look at ret_mean and sample_count.

Rising:
Usually good. The agent is collecting more reward per update.

Falling:
Usually bad if sustained. Could mean fewer kicks, weaker forward velocity, more
backward kicks, or too much time penalty.

Very unstable:
Common in RL. If the rolling average rises, instability is acceptable. If the
rolling average is flat while spikes appear, the policy may be relying on random
accidental kicks.

Important warning:
Stage 1 fixed-ball training can show much higher accumulated_reward than Stage 2
randomized-ball training because the task is easier and more repeatable.


sample_count
------------

Meaning:
Number of transitions used in this PPO update.

Calculated as:
buffer_sample_count = int(self.buf.ptr)

How to interpret:
This tells you how much data the PPO update used. With steps_per_env=512, many
updates should be near 512 if training happens when the buffer is full. If
training happens at episode endings or at 80 percent buffer fill, sample_count
may be lower.

Rising/falling:
Not directly good or bad. It mostly reflects episode lengths and when training
is triggered.

Problems:
Very low sample_count means updates are based on little data and will be noisy.
Large variation in sample_count makes accumulated_reward harder to compare
between rows.


clip_ratio
----------

Meaning:
PPO clipping threshold. This is a hyperparameter, not a learned result.

Calculated as:
self.clip_ratio, usually 0.2.

How to interpret:
PPO clips policy probability ratios outside [1 - clip_ratio, 1 + clip_ratio].
For clip_ratio=0.2, ratios outside [0.8, 1.2] are clipped in the policy loss.

Rising/falling:
Should be stable unless you manually change it.

Problems:
Too high can allow large, unstable policy changes. Too low can make learning
slow.


target_kl
---------

Meaning:
Target KL-divergence threshold used for early stopping.

Calculated as:
self.target_kl, usually 0.01 in this project.

How to interpret:
If approx_kl > 1.5 * target_kl, PPO can stop extra train iterations early.
With target_kl=0.01, early stop triggers around approx_kl > 0.015.

Rising/falling:
Should be stable unless manually changed.

Problems:
Too low can stop learning too often. Too high can allow policy jumps.

Note:
Current logs may not include target_kl because it is commented out in
update_metrics, but older reports do include it.


lr
--

Meaning:
Learning rate used by the Adam optimizer.

Calculated as:
self.optimizer.param_groups[0]["lr"] in older logs.

How to interpret:
Controls update size. Current default is 1e-4.

Rising/falling:
Usually stable unless you add scheduling.

Problems:
Too high: approx_kl, clip_frac, ratio_std, and grad_norm may spike; behavior may
change suddenly.
Too low: approx_kl and ratio_std may stay tiny; learning may be very slow.

Note:
Current logs may not include lr because it is commented out in update_metrics,
but older reports do include it.


log_std_mean
------------

Meaning:
Average log standard deviation of the Gaussian policy across action dimensions.
This controls exploration before the tanh squashing.

Calculated as:
mean(self.log_std)

The action is sampled as:
pre_tanh_action = mean + exp(log_std) * random_noise
action = tanh(pre_tanh_action)

How to interpret:
Convert log_std to standard deviation with exp(log_std).

Examples:
- log_std = -1.0  -> std approximately 0.368
- log_std = -1.1  -> std approximately 0.333
- log_std = -0.5  -> std approximately 0.607
- log_std = -2.0  -> std approximately 0.135

Rising:
The policy is becoming more random/exploratory. This can help early exploration,
but if it keeps rising while reward is not improving, the agent may be relying
on random actions.

Falling:
The policy is becoming more deterministic/confident. This is often expected
after the agent discovers a useful behavior.

Stable:
Can be fine. If stable near the initial value and learning is weak, exploration
may not be adapting much.

Problems:
Rising entropy/log_std with flat reward is suspicious. Falling too far too early
can also be bad because the agent stops exploring before it finds the behavior.


log_std_min
-----------

Meaning:
Smallest log_std among the action dimensions.

Calculated as:
min(self.log_std)

How to interpret:
Shows the least exploratory action dimension.

Rising:
Even the most deterministic action dimension is becoming more random.

Falling:
At least one action dimension is becoming more deterministic.

Problems:
If log_std_min is much lower than log_std_max, different action dimensions have
very different exploration levels. This can be normal, but inspect which action
dimension corresponds to rotation target, rotation speed, translation target,
or translation speed.


log_std_max
-----------

Meaning:
Largest log_std among the action dimensions.

Calculated as:
max(self.log_std)

How to interpret:
Shows the most exploratory action dimension.

Rising:
At least one action dimension is becoming more random.

Falling:
The most random action dimension is becoming more deterministic.

Problems:
If log_std_max rises while reward is poor, one action dimension may be noisy and
preventing stable control.


entropy_gauss
-------------

Meaning:
Entropy of the pre-tanh Gaussian policy. It is a proxy for policy randomness.
Higher entropy means more random/exploratory actions.

Calculated as:
sum over action dimensions of:
0.5 * (1 + log(2*pi)) + log_std

Because act_dim=4, entropy is affected by all four action dimensions.

How to interpret:
entropy_gauss mostly follows log_std_mean. If log_std_mean rises, entropy_gauss
rises. If log_std_mean falls, entropy_gauss falls.

Rising:
Policy is becoming more random. This can be useful early, but suspicious if the
agent should already be exploiting a learned skill.

Falling:
Policy is becoming more confident/deterministic. In successful Stage 1-style
training this is often expected.

Stable:
The exploration level is not changing much.

Problems:
- Rising entropy + rising reward: exploration may still be helping.
- Rising entropy + flat/noisy reward: policy may be failing to learn a precise
  behavior.
- Falling entropy + falling reward: policy may be prematurely collapsing to a
  bad behavior.

Important:
This is Gaussian entropy before tanh. The actual squashed action distribution is
not exactly measured by this metric.


loss_pi
-------

Meaning:
Policy loss from the PPO clipped objective.

Calculated as:
ratio = exp(logp_pi - logp_old)
obj = ratio * advantage
clipped_obj = clamp(ratio, 1 - clip_ratio, 1 + clip_ratio) * advantage
loss_pi = -mean(min(obj, clipped_obj))

How to interpret:
This is the actor/policy optimization objective. Lower is not always "better" in
the same way supervised learning loss is better. PPO policy loss depends on
normalized advantages and the current update, so it often hovers around zero.

Rising/falling:
Small changes around zero are normal.

Problems:
Large spikes can mean unstable advantages, unstable probability ratios, or too
large policy updates.

Important:
Do not use loss_pi alone to decide if the agent is learning. Use reward,
explained_variance, approx_kl, clip_frac, entropy, and actual behavior.


loss_v
------

Meaning:
Value-function loss. This measures how well the critic predicts returns.

Calculated as:
mean((ret - value_prediction)^2)

How to interpret:
Lower generally means the critic is fitting the returns better. A better critic
usually gives better advantage estimates and more stable policy learning.

Falling:
Usually good, especially if explained_variance also rises.

Rising:
The critic is fitting returns worse. This can happen when the policy discovers
new behavior and returns change, or when rewards are noisy.

Stable:
Can be fine, but check explained_variance.

Problems:
Low loss_v does not always mean a good critic. If return variance is tiny,
predicting near the mean can give a low MSE but still be unhelpful. Always check
explained_variance too.


loss_total
----------

Meaning:
Total loss optimized by the network.

Calculated as:
loss_total = loss_pi + 0.5 * loss_v + l2_lambda * l2_norm

where:
- loss_pi is the PPO policy loss
- loss_v is value loss
- l2_norm is sum of squared actor-critic parameters
- l2_lambda is currently 5e-4

How to interpret:
This is the scalar sent to backpropagation. It combines actor, critic, and L2
regularization.

Falling:
Often good, but not sufficient. It can fall even when behavior does not improve,
especially if value targets become easier/smaller.

Rising:
Can indicate unstable critic targets, larger policy updates, or changing reward
distribution.

Problems:
Do not treat this like supervised training loss. In RL, lower loss_total does
not guarantee better policy behavior.


approx_kl
---------

Meaning:
Approximate KL divergence between the old policy that collected the data and
the updated policy.

Calculated as:
mean(logp_old - logp_pi)

How to interpret:
Measures how much the policy changed during the update. PPO wants policy changes
to be limited.

Good range:
Usually below target_kl. With target_kl=0.01, values around 0.001-0.01 are
typically reasonable. Early stop happens around 0.015 in the current logic.

Rising:
Policy updates are getting larger.

Falling / near zero:
Policy updates are very small.

Problems:
- Very high: unstable updates, reduce lr or train_iters.
- Always almost zero: learning may be too conservative, or advantages are weak.
- Negative values can occur because this is an approximation/noisy sample
  estimate. Small negative values are not automatically a problem.


clip_frac
---------

Meaning:
Fraction of samples where PPO clipped the probability ratio.

Calculated as:
mean((ratio > 1 + clip_ratio) OR (ratio < 1 - clip_ratio))

With clip_ratio=0.2, this counts samples with ratio < 0.8 or ratio > 1.2.

How to interpret:
Shows how often PPO had to limit the policy update.

Rising:
More samples are being clipped, so updates are more aggressive.

Falling / zero:
Few or no samples are clipped, so policy changes are small.

Healthy values:
There is no exact perfect value, but constantly 0.0 can mean very small updates,
while very high values can mean unstable updates.

Problems:
- High clip_frac with high approx_kl: update too aggressive.
- Always zero with flat reward: policy may not be learning enough from data.


ratio_mean
----------

Meaning:
Average probability ratio between the new policy and old policy for sampled
actions.

Calculated as:
mean(exp(logp_pi - logp_old))

How to interpret:
Should usually stay close to 1.0. Ratio = 1 means the new policy assigns about
the same probability to the collected actions as the old policy.

Rising above 1:
New policy assigns higher probability to sampled actions on average.

Falling below 1:
New policy assigns lower probability to sampled actions on average.

Problems:
Large deviations from 1.0 mean the policy changed a lot. Check approx_kl and
clip_frac.


ratio_std
---------

Meaning:
Standard deviation of the probability ratio.

Calculated as:
std(exp(logp_pi - logp_old))

How to interpret:
Shows how uneven the policy change is across samples. Low ratio_std means most
samples changed similarly/little. High ratio_std means some samples changed much
more than others.

Rising:
Policy update is becoming more uneven/aggressive.

Falling:
Policy update is becoming smaller or more uniform.

Problems:
High ratio_std with high clip_frac or high approx_kl indicates unstable policy
updates. Very low ratio_std all the time may mean the policy barely changes.


grad_norm
---------

Meaning:
Global gradient norm for actor-critic parameters and log_std.

Calculated as:
sqrt(sum of squared gradients over all parameters with gradients)

How to interpret:
Shows update magnitude before optimizer.step(). It is useful for detecting
exploding or vanishing gradients.

Rising:
Gradients are getting larger. This can happen when reward/return targets become
larger or when the model is unstable.

Falling:
Gradients are getting smaller. This can happen as learning stabilizes, but also
if the model stops receiving useful learning signal.

Problems:
- NaN or inf: serious numerical instability.
- Huge spikes: possible unstable reward, value loss, or learning rate.
- Always extremely tiny: learning may be stalled.

Note:
This code logs grad_norm but does not clip gradients. If grad_norm spikes are a
problem, gradient clipping could be added.


explained_variance
------------------

Meaning:
How well the critic/value function explains the variance in the returns.

Calculated as:
1 - var(ret - v_pred) / var(ret)

How to interpret:
This is one of the most important critic diagnostics.

Approximate meaning:
- 1.0: critic predicts returns very well
- 0.0: critic is no better than predicting the average return
- negative: critic is worse than predicting the average return

Rising:
Good. The critic is learning to predict returns.

Falling:
Bad if sustained. The critic is becoming less useful.

Stable near 0:
The critic is not learning much useful structure.

Problems:
Low or negative explained_variance makes advantage estimates noisy. Noisy
advantages make PPO policy updates noisy. In your Stage 1 fixed-ball run,
explained_variance rose strongly, which matched successful learning. In some
Stage 2 runs, explained_variance stayed near zero, which suggests the critic did
not understand the harder/randomized task well.


ret_mean
--------

Meaning:
Average return target in the current PPO buffer.

Calculated as:
mean(ret)

Returns are computed in PPOBuffer.finish_path():
ret = advantage + value_prediction

The advantage is computed using GAE:
delta_t = reward_t + gamma * value_{t+1} - value_t
advantage_t = delta_t + gamma * lam * advantage_{t+1}

How to interpret:
This is the average target the critic is trying to predict.

Rising:
Usually good. The buffer contains better expected future reward.

Falling:
Usually bad if sustained. The buffer contains worse expected future reward.

Stable:
The average return level is stable.

Problems:
If ret_mean rises but accumulated_reward does not, inspect sample_count and
reward timing. If ret_mean is very small, loss values may also be small even if
behavior is not good.


ret_std
-------

Meaning:
Standard deviation of return targets in the current PPO buffer.

Calculated as:
std(ret)

How to interpret:
Shows how varied the return targets are. Higher ret_std means some states/actions
lead to much better or worse future reward than others.

Rising:
Returns are becoming more varied. This can be good if the agent is discovering
high-reward behavior, but it can also mean the task is noisy.

Falling:
Returns are becoming more uniform. This can happen when behavior stabilizes or
when all trajectories are similarly bad/good.

Problems:
Very low ret_std makes explained_variance less informative and can mean the
critic has little signal to learn from. Very high ret_std can make value learning
difficult.


Relationships between metrics
=============================

Reward vs loss
--------------

Reward is the main outcome metric. Loss is only an optimization diagnostic.

Good:
- accumulated_reward rolling mean rises
- explained_variance rises
- entropy/log_std slowly decreases or stabilizes
- loss_v decreases or stays controlled

Suspicious:
- loss_total falls but reward does not improve
- loss_v is low but explained_variance is near zero
- entropy rises while reward is flat


Entropy vs log_std
------------------

entropy_gauss and log_std_mean are directly linked. If log_std_mean rises,
entropy_gauss rises. If log_std_mean falls, entropy_gauss falls.

In successful fixed-ball training, it is normal for entropy to fall because the
agent finds a reliable action pattern.

In harder randomized-ball training, entropy may stay higher for longer because
the agent needs more exploration. But if entropy keeps rising and reward remains
poor, the policy may be becoming random instead of skillful.


approx_kl, clip_frac, ratio_std
-------------------------------

These three metrics describe policy update size.

Small update:
- approx_kl near 0
- clip_frac near 0
- ratio_std very low

Large/aggressive update:
- approx_kl high
- clip_frac high
- ratio_std high

If reward is not improving and all three are tiny, learning may be too weak.
If they are high and reward is unstable, learning may be too aggressive.


explained_variance and loss_v
-----------------------------

Use both together.

Good critic:
- explained_variance rises toward positive values
- loss_v is controlled or decreasing

Weak critic:
- explained_variance near 0 or negative
- loss_v may be low or high

Why loss_v alone is not enough:
If returns have low variance, MSE can be low even when the critic is not useful.
explained_variance tells whether the critic explains the structure in returns.


accumulated_reward and sample_count
-----------------------------------

accumulated_reward is a sum over the buffer. If sample_count changes, compare
reward per sample:

reward_per_sample = accumulated_reward / sample_count

This can make runs easier to compare when episodes end at different times or
buffer sizes differ.


Suggested interpretation workflow
=================================

1. Plot accumulated_reward rolling mean.
   If it trends upward, the run is probably learning something.

2. Plot reward_per_sample.
   This avoids being fooled by different sample_count values.

3. Check entropy_gauss and log_std_mean.
   Decide whether the policy is becoming more random or more deterministic.

4. Check explained_variance.
   If it stays near 0 or negative, the critic is weak and PPO updates are likely
   noisy.

5. Check approx_kl, clip_frac, and ratio_std.
   These show whether policy updates are too small or too aggressive.

6. Check actual simulator behavior.
   For this project, visual behavior matters. A curve can improve because of
   accidental contacts, but the real goal is controlled alignment and kicking.


Common patterns
===============

Pattern: Reward rising, entropy falling, explained_variance rising
------------------------------------------------------------------

Interpretation:
Healthy learning. The agent is getting better, becoming more confident, and the
critic is learning.

This was similar to the Stage 1 fixed-ball behavior.


Pattern: Loss falling, reward flat, explained_variance near 0
------------------------------------------------------------

Interpretation:
The optimizer is reducing the numeric objective, but the policy may not be
learning useful behavior. The critic may only be predicting average/small
returns.

Action:
Improve reward shaping, curriculum, observation correctness, or critic training.


Pattern: Entropy rising, reward noisy/flat
-----------------------------------------

Interpretation:
The policy is becoming more random. It may be searching, but it may also be
relying on accidental kicks.

Action:
Consider curriculum, alignment reward, freezing/clamping log_std, or simplifying
the action space.


Pattern: approx_kl and clip_frac always almost zero
--------------------------------------------------

Interpretation:
Policy updates are tiny.

Action:
Possibly increase train_iters, increase learning rate slightly, improve reward
signal, or check that advantages are meaningful.


Pattern: approx_kl high, clip_frac high, early_stop frequent
-----------------------------------------------------------

Interpretation:
Policy updates are too large.

Action:
Lower lr, lower train_iters, increase batch/sample_count, normalize rewards, or
check for reward spikes.


Pattern: explained_variance negative
------------------------------------

Interpretation:
The critic is worse than predicting the mean return.

Action:
Train critic more, lower learning rate if unstable, improve reward consistency,
increase buffer size, or simplify/curriculum the task.


Pattern: accumulated_reward has spikes but rolling mean is flat
--------------------------------------------------------------

Interpretation:
The agent occasionally gets lucky, but has not learned reliable behavior.

Action:
Check whether kicks occur when ball_rod_dist_y is near zero. If not, improve
observation/reward alignment.


Project-specific notes
======================

Stage 1 fixed-ball training
---------------------------

Fixed-ball training is much easier. The agent can learn a repeated movement
pattern. In that case, it is normal to see:

- reward rising strongly
- entropy falling
- log_std_mean becoming more negative
- explained_variance improving

This proves the PPO loop can learn a simple repeated motor primitive.


Stage 2 randomized-ball training
--------------------------------

Randomized ball positions are harder. The policy must use observations to align
the rod with the ball before kicking. In this setting, unstable reward and low
explained_variance can mean:

- the observation does not fully describe the useful state
- the reward is too sparse or mostly rewards accidental contact
- the curriculum jump from fixed ball to random ball is too large
- the critic cannot predict returns because the task is too variable
- exploration is too high or too low

For Stage 2, it is useful to log/plot:

- ball_y_world
- rod_y_world
- ball_rod_dist_y
- ball_kicked
- reward
- action components

If good kicks happen when ball_rod_dist_y is near zero, the observation is
probably meaningful. If kicks happen randomly with respect to ball_rod_dist_y,
the state/reward relationship still needs debugging.


Quick checklist for a new run
=============================

- Is accumulated_reward rolling mean rising?
- Is reward_per_sample rising?
- Is entropy_gauss/log_std_mean behaving as expected?
- Is explained_variance above 0 and improving?
- Is approx_kl below target_kl but not always exactly zero?
- Is clip_frac reasonable, not always huge and not always zero?
- Is ratio_mean close to 1.0?
- Is grad_norm finite without large spikes?
- Does the actual rod visually align with the ball before kicking?

If the answer to reward and behavior is yes, the run is useful even if some
metrics are noisy. If reward/loss look good but behavior looks random, trust the
behavior and inspect the reward/observation design.
